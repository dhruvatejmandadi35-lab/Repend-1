Module 01 · Lesson

What are we actually defending?

Before firewalls and passwords, one question: what does an attacker want, and what stands between them and it?

Every system holds something worth taking — data, access, or attention. Security isn't a wall; it's a set of trade-offs about how hard you make each of those to reach.

The CIA triad — Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability — is the lens every defense decision passes through.

Why it breaks

Most breaches aren't clever. They're a default password, an unpatched service, or a person clicking a link. The interesting part is the chain: one small gap becomes a foothold, a foothold becomes control.

In the lab, you'll watch a single packet try to reach a protected host — and see exactly where a firewall decides to let it through or drop it.
